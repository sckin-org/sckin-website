From October 15 to 17, 2026, SCKIN took part in the Sickle Cell Disease
Association of America's 54th Annual National Convention, at the Embassy
Suites by Hilton, Charlotte Concord Golf Resort & Spa in Concord, North
Carolina.

Our poster, **What do people ask SickleCellPedia, an AI assistant for
sickle cell disease?**, by Zacharie Liman-Tinguiri, Dr Lewis Thomas and
Matthew Agnello, looked at a sample of 1,925 messages that 434 people in
33 countries sent to SickleCellPedia on WhatsApp and the web between
June 2025 and October 2026.

## What we found

- **The top three needs** are complications (26% of conversations),
  understanding the disease (24%) and medicines (22%).
- **Questions get personal.** As conversations go on, people move from
  facts and medicine toward psychological and existential concerns. These
  make up 4% of first questions and 16% of questions from the fifth
  onward.
- **Most people asking are living with the disease or caring for someone
  who is.** Where people said who they were, 66% were patients and 29%
  family members or caregivers.

People ask an AI assistant questions they may not ask in clinic: sometimes
out of shame, often because no expert is available when the question comes
up. No patient can reach their doctor at any hour.

## How we did it

SickleCellPedia users accept a privacy policy that covers research use.
Conversations were anonymized before analysis, SCKIN's own test
conversations were excluded, and an AI language model classified each
question. These are preliminary, descriptive results from a sample.

SickleCellPedia is available to non-profit organizations on a
